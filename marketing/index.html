Measure the rule. Keep what pays.

We built a tool that measures whether a DNA coding rule actually pays off on your channel, and tunes the codec accordingly.

StrandAudit, an adaptive DNA codec tuner. Built at HackMIT 2026.

Rules chosen once, applied to every channel

DNA stores data at extreme density for centuries, but writing and reading it is noisy. Letters get swapped, added or dropped, and whole strands go missing. Storage pipelines cope with a few hand-written rules. Each rule costs density or reads. They were picked once, copied between papers, and are applied to every sequencing channel. Nobody measures whether they pay off.

Two tiers, one fixed target

Every setting is judged the same way: recover a fixed 20 KB file exactly in all 300 held-out trials, then count the reads per strand and bits per base it took. The encoder and decoder stay the same. Only the rules, the redundancy and the choice of candidate strands change.

Tier 1

Rule audit

Switch each rule off, one at a time, on one channel. Measure what it costs and what it buys at the recovery target. Tune the redundancy the same way. Keep only what pays off.

Tier 2

Learned selection

A Fountain encoder can write the same data as many different strands at no cost in density. A small CNN, trained on where the decoder actually fails on this channel, scores the candidates. The encoder keeps the safest one.

There is something to learn. On real Nanopore reads, errors that every read of a strand shares are largely predictable from the local 5-letter context, in contexts the hand rules don't cover:

  • CGGGsubstitutions around
  • CCCGsubstitutions around
  • CCCTdeletions right after
  • GGGAdeletions right after
  1. Adapt the decoderto this channel, then freeze it
  2. Label strandssimulate each 32 times, record how often decoding fails
  3. Train the risk modelon those failure rates
  4. Search settingsrules, redundancy, risk threshold; keep the cheapest that meets the target
  5. Re-adapt the decoderto the strands the new encoder writes

At most three rounds. Each round is one point on the Pareto plot of bits per base against reads per strand.

Sim-to-real firewall

The loop might learn a quirk of our simulator instead of a property of DNA. Three checks catch that.

  • Simulator A, held-out seedsThe calibrated simulator we optimize on, with seeds no training touched.
  • Simulator BSame channel built from different mechanisms: bursty errors, a different position curve, a context table from another dataset. Never used for optimization.
  • Real readsDecoder accuracy and the risk model's ranking on held-out real Nanopore clusters.
